Application for Asylum and for Withholding of Removal

Every box on the form, explained. Click any box and the camera flies in to tell you exactly what belongs there, what trips people up, and what a clean answer looks like.

I-589 is the form a person in the United States uses to ask for protection because they fear persecution or torture if sent back to their home country. One application asks for three kinds of protection at once: asylum, withholding of removal, and protection under the Convention Against Torture. It's shared by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services and the immigration courts, which is why both agencies appear at the top of the form.

01

Who files it

Anyone physically in the U.S., whatever their current status or how they entered. A spouse and unmarried children under 21 in the U.S. can usually be included on the same application.

02

Two paths

Affirmative: not in removal proceedings, so you file with USCIS and interview with an asylum officer. Defensive: already in immigration court, so you file with the immigration judge.

03

The clock

Asylum generally must be filed within one year of your last arrival, with narrow exceptions. Withholding and torture protection have no one-year deadline.

04

What it proves

Your story, in your words: what happened, who did it, and why, tied to race, religion, nationality, political opinion or a particular social group. Honesty matters; a frivolous application has permanent consequences.

An educational guide, not legal advice. Always check the current edition and instructions at uscis.gov/i-589.
